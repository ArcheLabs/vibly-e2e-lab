# Known Problems

## Project-Level Problems

The project lacks enough structure for agents to reliably build on one another's work.

Current project-level gaps:

- no structured literature index;
- no proof-attempt taxonomy;
- no failed-path archive;
- no shared review rubric;
- no artifact templates;
- no computational verification map;
- no partial-results map;
- no theorem dependency map;
- no clear promotion path from artifact to knowledge.

## Research-Level Problems

Goldbach-related research has several difficulties that agents must respect:

- numerical examples are not proofs;
- probabilistic models are not proofs;
- finite verification is not a general proof;
- partial results may be easy to overstate;
- proof routes can fail because of hidden assumptions;
- methods may be blocked by known obstructions;
- informal analogies can be misleading.

## Coordination Problems

Multi-agent research can fail even when individual outputs are plausible.

Known coordination risks:

- agents duplicate the same literature summary;
- observers propose broad tasks with no artifact;
- reviewers accept unsupported claims;
- artifacts use inconsistent terminology;
- failed attempts are discarded instead of archived;
- claim levels are not marked;
- knowledge entries are not tagged;
- tasks are not scoped tightly enough.

## Review Problems

Reviewer failure modes include:

- approving because the writing sounds confident;
- rejecting failure artifacts merely because they failed;
- failing to distinguish heuristic value from proof value;
- ignoring non-duplication checks;
- not requesting limitations;
- not checking whether the artifact can be reused;
- giving a score without an actionable reason.

## Knowledge Management Problems

The knowledge base must avoid becoming a pile of prose.

Known knowledge-management problems:

- repeated summaries without IDs or tags;
- no relation between entries;
- no source basis;
- no claim level;
- no limitation section;
- no next-step recommendation;
- no archive of rejected or failed routes.

## Open Obstacles

The project should eventually map mathematical obstacles such as:

- parity barriers in sieve-style reasoning;
- difficulty converting average estimates into exact representations;
- limitations of computational verification;
- gaps between heuristic density and deterministic proof;
- dependency on unproven assumptions;
- boundary-case handling.

These obstacles should be recorded carefully before they are used in evaluation.
