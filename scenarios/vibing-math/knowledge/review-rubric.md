# Review Rubric

## Review Goals

Reviews protect the research base.

A reviewer should not only ask whether an artifact is correct. The reviewer should ask whether it is useful, bounded, non-duplicative, and safe to promote into shared knowledge.

## Scoring Scale

```text
0 = Invalid or unsafe
1 = Weak, mostly unusable
2 = Partially useful but needs revision
3 = Acceptable
4 = Strong and reusable
5 = Excellent, durable research asset
```

## Required Review Fields

Every review should include:

```text
Decision:
Score:
Summary:
Strengths:
Weaknesses:
Claim level:
Source basis:
Limitations:
Risk:
Required revisions:
Knowledge promotion recommendation:
Next-step recommendation:
```

## Acceptance Criteria

An artifact can be accepted when it:

- satisfies the task;
- uses the required structure;
- states its claim level;
- avoids overclaiming;
- includes limitations;
- cites sources or project knowledge where needed;
- is reusable by future agents;
- adds non-duplicative value.

## Revision Criteria

Request revision when:

- the artifact is useful but underspecified;
- claim level is missing;
- limitations are unclear;
- source basis is incomplete;
- structure is not reusable;
- tags are missing;
- next step is unclear;
- the artifact duplicates prior knowledge but can be reframed.

## Rejection Criteria

Reject when:

- the artifact invents citations;
- the artifact claims a proof without support;
- the artifact ignores task scope;
- the artifact cannot be reviewed;
- the artifact hides assumptions;
- the artifact duplicates existing knowledge without improvement;
- the artifact treats a heuristic as a verified result;
- the artifact has no reusable output.

## High-Value Failure Criteria

A failed attempt can receive a high score when it:

1. clearly states the attempted claim;
2. explains the route;
3. lists key assumptions;
4. identifies the exact failure point;
5. records a reusable obstruction;
6. explains what future agents should avoid;
7. suggests a future retry condition;
8. is structured enough for the failed-path archive.

A well-documented failure may score 4 or 5 if it prevents repeated work or exposes an important obstruction.

## Exploration Bonus

Add positive weight when an artifact:

- proposes a useful new taxonomy;
- connects two previously separate methods;
- identifies a testable intermediate lemma;
- reframes a broad problem into smaller tasks;
- reveals an overlooked obstruction;
- creates a reusable template;
- improves the project's ability to search.

Exploration should not be rewarded for novelty alone. It must be bounded and reviewable.

## Red Flags

Reduce score for:

- claim inflation;
- unsourced mathematical claims;
- hidden assumptions;
- fake citations;
- proof by analogy only;
- no limitation section;
- no relation to project status;
- no non-duplication check;
- no next-step recommendation;
- overly broad conclusions.

## Knowledge Promotion Recommendation

Reviewers should recommend one of:

```text
Promote to knowledge
Promote after revision
Archive as failed path
Keep as task artifact only
Reject
```

The recommendation should explain why.
