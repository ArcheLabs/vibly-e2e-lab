# Goldbach Background

## Statement

Goldbach's conjecture states that every even integer greater than 2 can be expressed as the sum of two primes.

Example:

```text
10 = 3 + 7
28 = 5 + 23
100 = 47 + 53
```

This project focuses on research coordination around the conjecture and related problems. The initial phase is not a direct proof attempt. It is a structured effort to map known approaches, limitations, partial results, and useful research assets.

## Why It Is Difficult

The conjecture is simple to state but difficult because local numerical evidence does not automatically produce a general proof.

Challenges include:

- the irregular distribution of primes;
- the need for global control over all even integers;
- the gap between probabilistic expectation and proof;
- limitations of sieve methods, including parity-type barriers;
- the difficulty of converting density or average results into exact binary representations;
- the need to handle all large cases and all boundary cases.

## Known Directions

The project currently recognizes several broad directions:

- analytic number theory;
- circle-method-style reasoning;
- sieve methods;
- additive combinatorics;
- computational verification;
- partial results such as Chen-type theorems;
- heuristic and probabilistic models;
- conditional approaches under stronger assumptions.

These directions require structured treatment before agents should attempt major synthesis.

## Partial Results

Partial results may support the research map but should not be confused with the full conjecture.

Examples of partial-result categories:

- results for sums of more than two primes;
- results with one prime and one almost-prime;
- results for sufficiently large ranges under additional assumptions;
- density or average-case statements;
- computational verification up to a bound.

Each partial result should be recorded with its exact statement, assumptions, method, limitations, and relevance.

## Computational Verification

Computational verification can provide strong evidence over a finite range. It does not by itself prove the conjecture for all even integers.

A computational-verification artifact should record:

- verified range;
- algorithmic method;
- data source;
- reproducibility status;
- known limitations;
- whether the result is independently checkable;
- relation to theoretical work.

## Research Relevance for This Project

For Vibing Math agents, Goldbach is a suitable coordination target because it requires:

- long-horizon knowledge accumulation;
- precise claim discipline;
- literature synthesis;
- route comparison;
- failure memory;
- exploration of high-risk ideas;
- careful review.

This makes it a strong test case for agent-based research collaboration.

## Caution

Agents must avoid the following errors:

- presenting a heuristic as a proof;
- presenting a proof sketch as a verified result;
- presenting finite computation as a general proof;
- using unsourced claims about literature;
- ignoring known obstructions;
- omitting assumptions;
- hiding gaps in reasoning;
- claiming novelty without checking existing resources.

The correct posture is disciplined exploration.
